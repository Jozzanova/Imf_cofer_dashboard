# Reserves letter, 5 November 2026

This first letter covers the COFER release for the second quarter of 2026, published at the end of September, together with the gold and Norway figures on the page as of 13 October. The dollar's share of allocated reserves fell to 56.7 %, down 0.47 percentage points on the quarter, and almost all of that was central banks' own doing: at unchanged exchange rates the share would have fallen 0.62 points, with the dollar's rise against other currencies adding back 0.14. The euro took most of what the dollar lost.

## Currency composition

World foreign exchange reserves stood at USD 13,217bn at the end of June 2026, 0.9 % more than in March and 2.1 % more than a year earlier. The euro's share rose 0.56 points to 20.60 %, its highest in the five quarters shown, and the split says this was holdings, 0.69 points, against a small negative exchange-rate effect. The yen fell 0.47 points to 4.96 %, the lowest in the series, again mostly holdings. Sterling gained 0.16 points to 4.57 %, the renminbi 0.13 to 2.11 % and the Australian dollar 0.13 to 2.25 %; the Canadian dollar was flat at 2.47 %. Other currencies were 6.06 %, little changed on the quarter but 0.54 points higher than a year earlier.

Over the four quarters since mid-2025 the dollar is down 0.24 points, the euro up 0.34, the yen down 0.70 and sterling down 0.14. The IMF now estimates the composition of about 10.6 % of total reserves itself, so a tenth of the base behind these shares is the IMF's own estimate rather than reported figures.

## Gold

Gold's weight in reserves keeps climbing, on price rather than purchases. For the 91 countries that report both reserves and gold for the first quarter of 2026, the last quarter with a full panel, monetary gold was worth USD 4,031bn, 26.1 % of their total reserves against 19.7 % a year earlier. The gold price ended March at USD 4,863 per ounce, 12.7 % above December and 62.8 % above a year earlier; it had eased to 4,335 by September.

Buying was modest in the quarter: Poland added 31 tonnes, China 7, the Czech Republic, Australia and Malaysia about 5 each. Sales were larger: Kazakhstan 341 tonnes, Türkiye 118, Russia 22. Over four quarters Poland is the largest buyer at 85 tonnes, then Brazil 43, China 21 and the Czech Republic 20, and the reporters as a group are net sellers of about 193 tonnes. The largest holdings by value are the United States at USD 1,205bn, Germany 495bn, Italy 362bn, France 360bn, China 343bn and Switzerland 154bn.

## Norway

Norway's reserves were USD 91.2bn at the end of June 2026, 2.1bn or 2.3 % less than a year earlier, and equal to 14.0 % of this year's GDP. Norway holds no monetary gold. Among the 30 advanced economies it ranks 16th by the size of reserves and 11th by reserves relative to GDP. At year-ends the stock has moved in a narrow band: USD 75bn in 2020, 84bn in 2021, 72bn in 2022, 81bn in 2023 and 2024, 86bn in 2025. The largest advanced-economy holders are the United States at USD 1,307bn, Japan 1,287bn and Switzerland 1,087bn.

## Data status

COFER is complete through the second quarter of 2026; the third quarter is due at the end of December. The IMF's reserves and gold by country run to the second quarter for 62 reporters so far, so the gold share above is measured on the first quarter, where 91 countries have reported. Gold holdings in ounces run to the third quarter and the gold price to September. Seven countries that reported for the fourth quarter of 2025 have not yet reported for the first quarter of 2026.
